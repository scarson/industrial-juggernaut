### Two-regime agent-sensitivity distinction

The "no healthy config" result is not uniform across the grid — it splits into two regimes with opposite implications for what a stronger agent would change.

**Regime 1 — Small/dense boards (boardSize=96), turn-1 resolution: INTRINSIC GEOMETRY, not a weak-agent artifact.** On the 96-hex board, `radius=5` produces a base control disk that already covers roughly 6–10 iron hexes at setup vs `victoryThreshold`. The first mover seated by RNG controls a decisive fraction of iron before turn 1 even begins, resolving the game in turn 1. A stronger agent would win turn-1 MORE reliably, not less — the issue is geometric, not strategic. No agent improvement addresses this regime; it requires redesign (smaller radius, larger board, or higher victoryThreshold relative to ironCount).

**Regime 2 — Large boards (boardSize=220/300), cap-hit failures: AGENT-SENSITIVE.** The nearest-misses are all boardSize=300 configs that fail only on `capHitFraction > maxCapHit 0.02`. Cap-hits are no-winner games that ran to `turnCap=60` — the weak heuristic agent cannot convert positions decisively. A more decisive agent (paused MCTS) could plausibly convert these stalemates into iron-victories, pulling the cap-hit rate under the 0.02 threshold. The best near-miss (`big300`: boardSize=300, radius=5, ironCount=16, victoryThreshold=12) fails on capHit alone at the 360-game hi-res stage.

**Important nuance on Regime 2:** The two-regime split is directional, not absolute. Examining the grid table for boardSize=300 configs: roughly 45% of their failures occur WITHOUT a capHit violation — they fail on seatBias, leadVolatility, or ironVictory. Do not read the large-board regime as "all failures are cap-hit." The nearest-misses that surface in the top-3 happen to be cap-hit cases, but the regime is not monolithic.

**Scope implication:** "No healthy config under weak play" is narrower than "the game has no balanced region." The large-board cap-hit regime is the only corner where a stronger agent may reveal a passing config. The small-board geometric regime is a genuine design issue independent of agent quality.

### seatWinBias inflation caveat

`seatWinBias` measures win-rate differences across seating groups — but on high-capHit large boards, no-winner games reduce the win-rate denominator for all players, which can inflate apparent seatBias. This is documented behavior in metrics.ts (no-winner games are counted in `gamesPlayed` but not in any player's `wins`). Elevated `seatWinBias` on configs with high `capHitFraction` (particularly the boardSize=300 configs) is partly an artifact of this denominator effect; it should not be over-read as pure seating imbalance. The correct diagnostic is to re-run under a stronger agent that converts cap-hit games to decisive wins — which simultaneously addresses the capHit and the inflated seatBias.

## Recommendation and Conclusion

**No config from this sweep can be adopted as-is.** Zero of 176 feasible configs passed all health thresholds under the weak `heuristicAgent`. This finding stands; it is not a measurement error.

**The directional lever is clear: higher `victoryThreshold` + larger board.** The top nearest-misses are all boardSize=300, radius=5, victoryThreshold=12, ironCount=14–16. That is the search region that comes closest to health.

**The DECISIVE next step is a targeted MCTS re-run before any other decision.** The only config failing on a single criterion (nearest miss #1: `big300` — boardSize=300, radius=5, ironCount=16, victoryThreshold=12) fails solely on `capHitFraction 0.039 > 0.02`. CapHit is the metric most sensitive to agent strength: it counts games where the weak agent cannot break a stalemate within 60 turns. A stronger (paused MCTS) agent would convert those stalemates to decisive iron-victories, likely pulling the cap-hit rate under the threshold. The recommended MCTS re-run targets:

- **Primary:** `big300` (boardSize=300, radius=5, ironCount=16, victoryThreshold=12) — one failing criterion, cap-hit only.
- **Secondary:** radius=4, victoryThreshold=10/12, boardSize=220 and 300 configs — the next-closest large-board candidates, also cap-hit dominated.

**Do NOT loosen `maxCapHit` before the MCTS re-run.** Loosening the cap-hit threshold would mask a real signal (non-terminating games under weak play) rather than address it. The correct diagnostic is agent-strength, not threshold relaxation.

**Do NOT call the game unbalanced as a whole yet.** The two-regime analysis above shows the small-board failures are geometric, not agent-sensitive. The large-board failures are agent-sensitive in the cap-hit dimension. Both are resolvable — but they require different interventions, and neither has been fully explored under a strong agent. Calling the game structurally unbalanced at this stage would overclaim from a weak-agent run.

**If the MCTS re-run still yields no pass:** then capHit-threshold loosening (with explicit rationale), boardSize/radius redesign, or victoryThreshold extension become appropriate next options. None of those decisions should precede the MCTS run.
