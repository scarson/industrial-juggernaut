**INDUSTRIAL JUGGERNAUT**

*A Strategy Game of Industrial Expansion*

2–6 Players · Rules Draft v9

# Definitions
- **Turn.** One complete cycle of player Rounds. Play order is random each turn.

- **Round.** Each player’s opportunity to Build or Attack in the turn.

- **Factory:** A playing piece that can create new bases or new factories.

- **Base:** A playing piece that defines a player’s area of control and can attack opponent’s bases.

- **Resource count.** The number of iron hexes or factories under your control.

- **Under Control.** In the early part of the game, players have hexes and factories *under their control* if they are within a 5-hex range of their bases. Later in the game, players have hexes and factories *under their control* if they are within the player’s perimeter.

- **Perimeter:** The polygon formed by a player's outermost bases. Any hex touched by a straight line between the center of any two Perimeter Bases is considered within the perimeter.

- **Farthest Base:** The base farthest from a player's first (or oldest) base.

# Overview
Industrial Juggernaut is a 2–6 player strategy game played on a large hex map of a fictional continent. Players build industrial economies by controlling iron hexes, converting that capacity into factories and bases, and using military force to overtake rival nations. The first player — or alliance — to have 10 iron hexes under their control at the end of a round wins.

# Components
- **36 Factory tokens.** Gray, factory-shaped pieces

- **72 Base tokens.** Colored, tower-shaped pieces with a Fresh face and a Fatigued face (12 per player in 6 colors — White, Black, Blue, Red, Green, Gold)

- **48 Battle tokens.** Colored tokens used to determine the victor in a battle (8 per player in their color)

- **6 Turn Order tokens.** Tokens numbered 1–6 to randomly determine playing order each turn. Battle and Turn Order tokens are indistinguishable by touch.

- **Velvet bag.**

- **Continental Map playing board.**

# The Map
The board is an oval landmass of hex tiles. Fourteen hexes have iron deposits, which contribute to production and determine victory.

| **Hex Type** | **Count (approx.)** | **Gameplay Role**                        |
|--------------|---------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Total hexes  | 96 hexes            | Oval-shaped board                        |
| Iron hexes   | 14 hexes            | Counts toward Resource Count and Victory |

Iron hexes are distributed randomly across the interior of the board. No Iron appears in the two outermost rings of hexes. No two iron hexes may be placed so that either touches more than one other iron hex — groups of three or more adjacent iron hexes are not permitted.

# Setup
- Place the board in the center of the table.

- Each player selects a color and takes their 12 base tokens and 8 battle tokens.

- Place all 36 factory tokens in a central supply available to all players.

- Each player places their first base on any hex in the outermost ring of hexes near where they are sitting.

- Place as many Turn Order tokens in the velvet bag as there are players.

# Perimeter
Your Perimeter (or territory) is defined entirely by the position of your bases. Territory under your control may include iron hexes and factory pieces, which are key resources.

<u>Radiating Bases</u>. At the beginning of the game, when you have placed only 1, 2, or 3 bases, each base radiates a circle of control 5 hexes in every direction. the territory under your control is the combined area of those circles.

<u>Setting the Perimeter</u>. When you place your 4th base, your bases stop radiating out. Territory becomes every hex enclosed within the polygon formed by your four bases. If you are later knocked back below 4 bases, you return to the radius model until you rebuild your perimeter. It is possible to have fewer hexes under your control after you place your 4<sup>th</sup> base than you had with 3 bases. A perimeter must contain at least 1 iron hex (*see* Territory).

| **Bases Placed** | **Territory Perimeter** |
|----|----|
| 1 | 5-hex radius semi-circle from your first base at the edge of the board |
| 2 | Union of both 5-hex circles |
| 3 | Union of all three circles |
| 4+ | Everything enclosed within the perimeter of your outermost bases. Bases no longer radiate. |

## Overlapping Radii
When neither adjacent player has a perimeter (they have 3 or fewer bases), their radius circles may overlap. Both players are credited with iron hexes and factories that reside in the intersection of rival circles. Once a player establishes a perimeter, any resources within that perimeter is no longer available to adjacent players that are still radiating.

## Perimeters Cannot Overlap
Two players' perimeters cannot touch or cross. Because you cannot place a new base within an opponent's perimeter line, perimeters often have one or more hexes between them.

# Turn Order
At the start of each turn, players draw to determine the order of their rounds. Each player keeps their drawn token visible in front of them throughout the turn. Only after every player has completed their round are the Turn Order tokens returned to the velvet bag.

## 3–6 Player Game
For the very first turn, place all turn order tokens in the velvet bag and everyone draws randomly.

On subsequent turns, place only tokens #1 and #2 in the velvet bag. The two players who played last and second-to-last in the previous turn draw first. Then place remaining tokens in the velvet bag and all other players draw to define the order.

## 2 Player Game
Instead of turn order tokens, each player places battle tokens into the velvet bag equal to the number of iron hexes currently in their control. whoever’s token is drawn from the velvet bag plays first that turn.

# Player Round
On your round you do one of two things: Build or Attack.

## Build Action
You can build and place new factories or new bases depending on your Resource Count.

The calculation for resources is:

> ***# of iron hexes under your control <u>plus</u> # of factory pieces under your control***

You may build one Factory or one Base for every two resources, rounding down. If your resource count is 6, you may build and place 3 factories or 3 bases. After establishing resource count, you must decide whether you are building factories or bases.

**Bootstrapping.** At the beginning of the game, players will have zero Factories and may not have two iron hexes under their control. So long as a player has one iron hex under their control, they can build one factory. This applies at the start of the game and any time you have been knocked back below 4 bases.

| **Resource Count** | **Numberof Factories or Bases you can build** |
|:--:|----|
| 1 | 1 (if your Territory is defined by radiating Bases (\<4), 1 Factory can be placed if you have only 1 Iron hex and no Factories) |
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 1 |
| 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 2 |
| 6 | 3 |
| 7 | 3 |
| etc. | half, rounded down |

## Placing Factories or Bases
Factory and Base pieces are placed in the center of a single hex. Only one piece may be on a hex. A piece may not be placed on an Iron hex. Once placed, a Factory or Base is never moved, but it can be removed. When all pieces are placed on the board, the Round is over and the next player plays.

## Placing Factories
New Factories must be placed within 5 hexes of your Farthest Base — the base that is farthest from your first (oldest) Base. This prevents players from building factories far behind their own lines. Factories are not really “yours”. Like iron hexes, you use them when they are within your perimeter, and lose access when they are outside your perimeter.

> *⚑ There are only 36 Factories in the game. Every factory you place is one fewer available to the table.*

## Placing Bases
when the game begins, very little of the board is under anyone’s control. placing bases outside your perimeter is how you expand your territory into unclaimed hexes.

A new base placed outside your existing perimeter must satisfy all three of the following:

- <u>It must be within 5 hexes of an existing friendly base</u>.

- <u>It must not be placed within an opponent's perimeter</u> — beyond the line drawn between two opponent bases

- <u>It must create at least one unobscured triangle with two of your existing Bases</u> — meaning the new base can “see” the two others via straight lines drawn from the center of the new Base to the center of two existing Bases without crossing or intersecting any hex of an opponent's perimeter. Use a straight edge to check. The new base and two more existing bases form a triangle or polygon that extends your perimeter. New bases can often see more than two bases, meaning more triangles and more area under control

> *⚑ A base that is only visible to one existing base does not extend your perimeter because it encloses no new territory. Such placement is not allowed.*

When placing multiple bases, each new base changes the perimeter by creating one or more triangles with two other visible bases. When players have a high resource count, they can place multiple bases, expanding their perimeter by as many as 5 hexes with each base, claiming considerable area in a single turn.

Bases placed within your existing perimeter fortify your perimeter, but do not claim new territory.

**Attack Action**

Attacking is how a player expands territory at the expense of an opponent. A successful attack removes an opponent's base and replaces it with one of the attacker's own bases, changing both players' perimeters.

Combat Requirements

- Only bases that make up the outside of the opponent’s perimeter may be targeted. Bases lying within an opponent's perimeter may not be attacked.

- The attacker may commit 3, 4, 5, or 6 bases to the attack. Allied bases may count toward this total (*see* Alliances).

- All attacking bases must be within 6 hexes of the targeted base.

- The defender must commit exactly 1 base to the targeted base’s defense, also within 6 hexes of the targeted base.

**Combat Resolution**

Battle tokens are put in the velvet bag for each side. Draw 1 token — whoever's token is drawn wins. Every base committed to the attack or defense is Fatigued as its battle token is placed in the velvet bag.

The attacker chooses how many Bases to commit, which determines the tokens placed in the bag and the probability of winning:

| Attacker Bases Committed | Percentage Chance of Winning | Number of Attacker Tokens Placed in Velvet bag | Number of *Defender* Tokens Placed in Velvet bag |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 3 Bases | 75%\` | 3 Tokens | 1 Token |
| 4 Bases | 83% | 5 Tokens | 1 Token |
| 5 Bases | 89% | 8 Tokens | 1 Token |
| 6 Bases | 100% | Automatic | \- |

*Note: the number of tokens in the bag is sometimes greater than the number of bases committed, as higher commitment levels use additional tokens to increase win probability.*

At commitment level 6, no draw is needed — the attacker wins automatically.

**Fresh and Fatigued:**

Bases have two sides: Fresh and Fatigued. Bases start each turn fresh with that side faced away from the player. All bases that are involved in an attack or the defense are fatigued and cannot be used again that turn. They are rotated 180 degrees, so their fatigued side faces away from their player. At the start of the next turn, players rotate their bases back to fresh.

**Victory:**

If you win against a base on your opponent’s perimeter, you replace the defeated base with one of your own. The opponent's perimeter shrinks immediately, and your new base expands your perimeter. If the change in perimeter brings any iron hexes or factories into your perimeter, that factory is now under your control and no longer available to your opponent. Occasionally the swap of bases will leave resources outside of both player’s perimeters.

**Perimeter Reassessment:**

The loss of a Base temporarily breaks the perimeter. The player examines their remaining bases and determines a new perimeter based on the triangles between surviving bases. The shape of a perimeter can get very odd after losing a battle.

**Stranded Bases:**

When a perimeter is reassessed after a battle, one or more of the losing player's bases may find themselves visible to only one other friendly base. A base in this situation is called Stranded — it is no longer part of the perimeter and encloses no territory.

A stranded base may be rescued. If the player places a new base during their round (this turn o next) that gives the stranded base visibility to two friendly bases, it rejoins the perimeter and encloses territory normally.

While a base is stranded, opponents may place new bases behind it — since it lies outside its player's perimeter, and normal placement restriction do not apply. If an opponent places bases that fully encircle a stranded base, the stranded base is immediately removed from the board.

**Catastrophic Perimeter Loss:**

If losing the attack causes your number of bases to fall below 4 bases, your perimeter collapses entirely and your bases return to the radius model. Later in the game, when there are 18 or more factories placed on the board, perimeter loss ends that player’s game.

**Maxed out Bases**

If all of your 12 of Base pieces are already on the board at the time of a successful attack, you may pick up any one of your 12 bases and move it to replace the defeated base *so long as your newly defined perimeter intersect with any opponent’s perimeter*. Note that removing a base will shrink your perimeter on that side and may and put factories or iron hexes outside of your perimeter. Rather than move your 12<sup>th</sup> Base, you may opt to merely destroy the defeated base with no replacement.

**If the defender wins:**

Nothing changes other than fatigue.

**Multiple Attacks:**

If you have 6 or more Bases within range of opponent’s bases, you may declare multiple attacks in one round. Depending on the outcome of the first battle, you may to commit to a second or subsequent attack, including attacking the same base again (which survived the first attack) with a different set of your bases. Your attack round ends when you have fewer than 3 fresh bases, or at any time if you wish to save bases for possible defense.

**Base Limits and Player Elimination:**

- Each player starts with 12 bases.

- When you destroy a player's last base, their game is over, and you claim all their 12 base tokens.

**Alliances:**

Alliances may be formed at any time by mutual agreement and dissolved unilaterally. Alliance members may agree to loan their in-range bases in a combined attack, so long as the total bases involved does not exceed 6 in attack. If an alliance destroys a player’s last base, the player who called the attack gets the 12 defeated bases. The combined iron hex count of all alliance members counts toward the victory threshold.

# Ways to be defeated
No Bases. When an opponent destroys your final base, your game is over.

Broken Perimeter at 18 Factories. When 18 or more factories have been placed on the board, you are removed from the game when your perimeter is broken (you fall to 3 or fewer bases).

No Iron. If through a loss in battle or the creation of an adjacent opponent’s perimeter a player no longer has an iron hex under their control, their game is over.

Empty Perimeter. A player can destroy *themselves* by absentmindedly creating a perimeter that contains no iron hex when they place their 4<sup>th</sup> Base.

When a player is eliminated in these ways, all their remaining bases are given to whomever caused the destruction. if the player destroys themselves via an empty perimeter, their bases are given to no one.

# Winning
The game ends when any player — or alliance — controls 10 or more iron hexes at the end of a round.

# Strategy Notes
Iron is everything. It fuels your resource count and is your victory condition. Every decision should be evaluated against one question: does this move help me control more iron, or deny iron to rivals? You need to gain firm control of iron hexes early, since failure to have an iron hex in your perimeter automatically ends your game.

The shared factory supply is a weapon. A player who accumulates factories early can constrains everyone else because more factories can build even more factories.

If you place factories before you define your perimeter, it is wise to place your first factories on just one side of the line between your first two bases, since the 4-base perimeter will enclose only one side of that line.

Consider carefully the moment you place your 4<sup>th</sup> base. If you can build many factories before setting your perimeter you can create many bases at once. However, wait too long to create a perimeter and you will be overrun by another’s expanding line of bases.

Going first in a turn gives you the initiative. But consider holding some bases in reserve — players taking later rounds that same turn can attack you, and you may want defenders available.

Base placement shape is a strategic choice. An L-shape for your first three bases lets your 4th enclose a large rectangle — good for containing iron and factories.

Alliances are powerful but temporary. Two players combining Iron counts can reach 10 quickly. But alliances can be dissolved — or betrayed. The player who manages alliances well, knowing when to cooperate and when to strike, often wins games that pure military players lose.
